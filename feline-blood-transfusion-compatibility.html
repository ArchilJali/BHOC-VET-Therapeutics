Feline medicine · Donor welfare

Cats are different: blood compatibility and donor welfare.

Blood compatibility for the patient and protection for the healthy donor.

BHOC Veterinary · 3 Oct 2026

Feline blood transfusion compatibility

Cats have particular transfusion needs. Their A, B and AB blood types, naturally occurring alloantibodies and additional red-cell antigens make compatibility a clinical concern even before a first transfusion. A mismatched transfusion can be life-threatening. Blood typing and crossmatching matter.

ISFM feline transfusion guidelines (2021)

Cat blood donation and donor welfare

Donor welfare matters too. Blood donation places demands on a healthy cat: handling, restraint or sedation, venous access and blood removal. Individual temperament and health therefore matter. Sedation and blood collection each require a clinical risk assessment. Feline donors need careful selection, stress reduction and monitoring before, during and after collection.

ISFM feline transfusion guidelines (2021)

Feline blood donor sedation

Feline donation is usually performed under sedation. A 2024 study of 29,201 donation events reported that 95.6% were performed under sedation. Finding a suitable donor and compatible blood takes preparation, time and veterinary resources, especially when a critically ill cat needs urgent support.

Feline blood donation study (2024)

Historical Oxyglobin use in cats

EveryCat's Oxyglobin Use in Cats report, published on 17 November 2008, brings together historical experience with a hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier in feline anemia care. It cites a retrospective study of 48 cats, a clinical review and an earlier 72-cat case series. These are historical Oxyglobin publications, rather than new BHOC clinical results.

Read the EveryCat report

Why this matters

The recipient needs compatible blood. The healthy donor also needs protection. These two responsibilities motivate investigation of additional oxygen-delivery approaches that may reduce dependence on donor animals. Historical Oxyglobin evidence is product-specific and does not establish safety or efficacy for BHOC.
