Severe Anemia in a Dog With Leishmaniasis

A dog was found on the street in very poor condition and immediately brought to a veterinary clinic. The case record identifies leishmaniasis and severe anemia.

18 November 2026 · By Archil JaliashviliInitial hematology report dated 10 May 2024.

The dog was found outdoors in very poor condition and was immediately brought to a veterinary clinic. The case record identifies leishmaniasis and severe anemia. The clinic photograph is used as the lead image to document the patient receiving veterinary care.

The initial hematology report is dated 10 May 2024. It records hemoglobin (HGB) 65, red blood cell count (RBC) 2.78 and hematocrit (HCT) 19.4%. These values are transcribed from the supplied laboratory photograph. Units are not added because they are not clearly legible in the image.

During emergency veterinary care, BHOC (Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier) was administered twice to support oxygenation. This is the treatment description confirmed for publication. No dose volume is stated because the supplied record does not clearly establish the volume per administration or the total.

Canine leishmaniosis can be associated with hematological abnormalities, including anemia. The clinical significance of an individual blood count must be interpreted alongside examination findings and disease-specific testing. The supplied record identifies leishmaniasis; this article does not independently re-diagnose the dog from the photograph or blood count.

This case report documents the clinical presentation, the initial hematology values and the two BHOC administrations intended to support oxygenation. No post-treatment laboratory series, follow-up examination or clinical outcome has been supplied for this report. It therefore makes no claim that BHOC treated leishmaniasis or established a clinical outcome.

Case documentation and evidence boundary

Case photographs and the initial hematology report were supplied by Archil Jaliashvili. Initial report date: 10 May 2024. The diagnosis is recorded in the case history as leishmaniasis.

The owner confirmed that BHOC was administered twice to support oxygenation. The report does not establish a per-administration or total volume. Units for the CBC values are not clearly legible in the photograph.

This is an individual investigational case record. It does not establish product efficacy, causality, regulatory approval or a treatment protocol.
