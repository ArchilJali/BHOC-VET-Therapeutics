A Lion, a Tiger and a Bear: Friendship Beyond Species and Time

Archil JaliashviliBHOC Species & Biodiversity Protection Initiative26 October 2026

Leo the lion, Shere Khan the tiger and Baloo the bear grew up together at Noah's Ark Animal Sanctuary. Their friendship left a lesson that outlives them.

  • Friendship
  • Wildlife rescue
  • Shared biology
  • Respect for life

Three rescued cubs. One family.

In 2001, Leo, an African lion, Shere Khan, a Bengal tiger, and Baloo, an American black bear, were rescued from an Atlanta basement during a police raid. The neglected cubs were taken to Noah's Ark Animal Sanctuary in Locust Grove, Georgia, United States.

They became known as BLT: Bear, Lion, Tiger. They grew up in the same habitat, playing, resting and seeking affection from one another. Their bond continued into adulthood.

Baloo needed surgery to remove a harness that had grown into his skin. Sanctuary curator Allison Hedgecoth told ABC that Leo and Shere Khan became agitated while he was away. After the operation, Baloo returned to his companions.

Leo died in 2016 and Shere Khan in 2018. Baloo, the last surviving member of the trio, died on 6 May 2025 after veterinarians made the decision to euthanize him following declining health. The three had shared around 15 years together. Their story continues to bring people together.

Watch BLT on the sanctuary's official Facebook pageView the sanctuary's original BLT photograph

Friendship knows no species. Its legacy has no time limit.

Three rescued wild animals gave people a lesson in acceptance, trust and care. A lion, a tiger and a bear found companionship in one another. Their differences became part of the family they formed.

For us, their story carries a clear message: respect for life reaches across species. The care we give and the bonds we build can leave a meaning that lasts beyond a lifetime.

Nature gave us a shared architecture.

Lions, tigers, bears and humans are mammals. Hemoglobin shares a conserved oxygen-binding architecture across mammals, built around iron-containing heme groups. Each species adapts oxygen transport to its own physiology. The oxygen molecule is the same.

This shared biological foundation guides our work with BHOC, Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier, within Precision Oxygen Therapeutics. We are developing approaches to support oxygen delivery in veterinary care, including companion animals, wildlife and endangered species.

Understanding the biology we share gives us a responsibility: use science to protect life, with care for the needs of every species.
