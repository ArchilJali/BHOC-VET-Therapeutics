Three Years of BHOC Veterinary Research in Georgia

From our first investigational cases to three years of veterinary clinical experience with dogs and cats.

BHOC Veterinary · Precision Oxygen Therapeutics

Three years ago, BHOC began an important chapter of its veterinary research and development program in Georgia.

Among our earliest and most important clinical partners has been Perfect Clinic – AB-Vet Buneba, together with PhD David Nanobashvili and its experienced veterinary team.

What started as investigational work with dogs and cats has developed into three years of practical veterinary experience, clinical observation, scientific learning and continuing development.

During this period, BHOC has been evaluated in real veterinary clinical settings, including difficult cases in which oxygen delivery and severe anemia represented major clinical challenges.

From early cases to long-term clinical experience

Our original objective was to explore how a cell-free hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier could contribute to veterinary oxygen-support strategies when conventional donor blood may be unavailable, delayed or difficult to access.

Over time, the work became much more than a single study or an individual case. It developed into a continuing collaboration between scientists and practicing veterinarians — observing, learning, documenting and improving our understanding of oxygen delivery in veterinary medicine.

Most importantly, behind every data point was an animal patient.

During these three years, veterinarians at AB-Vet Buneba have treated animals facing serious clinical challenges, and the experience generated through these cases has contributed to the continuing development of BHOC Veterinary and Precision Oxygen Therapeutics.

A Georgian veterinary partnership

We extend our sincere appreciation to PhD David Nanobashvili and the entire team at Perfect Clinic – AB-Vet Buneba.

Their clinical expertise, professionalism, dedication to their patients and willingness to explore investigational approaches have made them important partners in the evolution of the BHOC Veterinary program.

Georgia has therefore become more than a location where early veterinary work was performed. It is one of the places where the practical clinical foundation of Precision Oxygen Therapeutics in veterinary medicine continues to evolve.

From experience to evidence

The next phase is clear. We intend to translate these years of practical experience into increasingly structured scientific work:

  • clinical documentation and case reports;
  • structured studies;
  • scientific publications;
  • veterinary education;
  • international research collaboration; and
  • new species-specific applications.

BHOC — Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier — is being developed as a cell-free hemoglobin-based oxygen-delivery platform within the broader field of Precision Oxygen Therapeutics.

It is being investigated as a complementary oxygen-delivery approach rather than as a replacement for whole blood or red blood cells.

Three years ago, we took some of our first veterinary steps in Georgia. Today, we have three years of experience behind us — and a much larger scientific journey ahead.
