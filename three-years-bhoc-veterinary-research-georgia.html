Three Years of BHOC Veterinary Research in Georgia

From early investigational cases to three years of veterinary clinical experience with dogs and cats in Georgia.

10 Nov 2026 · By Archil JaliashviliVeterinary research collaboration in Georgia: three years of clinical experience and development.

Three years ago, BHOC began an important chapter of its veterinary research and development program in Georgia.

Among our earliest and most important clinical partners has been Perfect Clinic – AB-Vet Buneba, together with PhD David Nanobashvili and its experienced veterinary team.

What started as investigational work with dogs and cats has developed into three years of practical veterinary experience, clinical observation, scientific learning and continuing development.

During this period, BHOC has been evaluated in real veterinary clinical settings, including difficult cases in which oxygen delivery and severe anemia represented major clinical challenges.

Over time, the work became much more than a single study or an individual case. It developed into a continuing collaboration between scientists and practicing veterinarians — observing, learning, documenting and improving our understanding of oxygen delivery in veterinary medicine.

Most importantly, behind every data point was an animal patient. The experience generated through these cases has contributed to the continuing development of BHOC Veterinary and Precision Oxygen Therapeutics.

We extend our sincere appreciation to PhD David Nanobashvili and the entire team at Perfect Clinic – AB-Vet Buneba for their clinical expertise, professionalism, dedication to their patients and long-term partnership.

Georgia has become one of the places where the practical clinical foundation of Precision Oxygen Therapeutics in veterinary medicine continues to evolve.

The next phase is to translate these years of practical experience into structured clinical documentation, case reports, studies, scientific publications, veterinary education, international research collaboration and new species-specific applications.

BHOC — Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier — is being developed as a cell-free hemoglobin-based oxygen-delivery platform within the broader field of Precision Oxygen Therapeutics. It is being investigated as a complementary oxygen-delivery approach rather than as a replacement for whole blood or red blood cells.

Three years ago, we took some of our first veterinary steps in Georgia. Today, we have three years of experience behind us — and a much larger scientific journey ahead.

Research status and interpretation

This article describes three years of investigational veterinary work and clinical experience in Georgia. It is not a claim of regulatory approval for any species or indication.

The collaboration includes practical clinical observation in dogs and cats. Individual cases do not by themselves establish product safety, efficacy, causality or a universal treatment protocol.

The next phase is to convert accumulated experience into structured documentation, studies, scientific publications, veterinary education and broader research collaboration.
