Historical veterinary precedent

Oxyglobin and canine anemia.

In 1998, the FDA approved Oxyglobin, hemoglobin glutamer-200 (bovine), for anemia in dogs. This is a product-specific veterinary HBOC precedent.

The historical approval does not establish BHOC approval or suitability in other species. Each application requires its own evidence for dosing, safety and efficacy.

Historical canine use map

What historical canine use mapped out.

Biopure's historical booklet reports 885 manufacturer-collected veterinary submissions from 1998-1999 and prints 146 selected canine case vignettes. It is a descriptive dog-only archive, separate from the controlled FDA trial.

Emergency blood loss and trauma

Historically reported as immediate oxygen-carrying support while bleeding control, diagnostics or surgery addressed the cause.

Perioperative anemia and hemorrhage

Historically reported before, during or after surgery when rapid oxygen-delivery support was clinically important.

Hemolysis and regenerative anemia

Historically reported as a bridge while disease-specific treatment acted; the archive also records deaths and later complications.

Ineffective erythropoiesis

Historically reported in renal, marrow and neoplastic disease, with mixed and often short follow-up.

Historical manufacturer-collected reports. Canine, product-specific and descriptive. They do not establish BHOC efficacy, approval in another species or current Oxyglobin availability.

BHOC Veterinary Therapeutics, also known as BHOC Vet, is led by Archil Jaliashvili. The BHOC Species & Biodiversity Protection Initiative connects oxygen delivery science with the future of animal health and species protection.

Our foundation is comparative biology: different species have different blood group systems, yet most vertebrates share hemoglobin-based oxygen transport. We explore how this foundation can inform species-specific research and development.
